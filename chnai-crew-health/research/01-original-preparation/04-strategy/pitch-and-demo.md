Two different audiences see two different things. The content is written on the weekend;
this is the shape.

## The 30-second demo

Thirty seconds is about 70 spoken words. One idea.

| Seconds | What is on screen |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 5 | The problem in one line: far from Earth, the astronaut is their own first doctor |
| 5 to 22 | The product, working: something is gathered, something is evaluated, an action appears |
| 22 to 27 | What is under it: NASA's limits and NASA's data, named |
| 27 to 30 | The name and the link |

Rules:

- Screen recording of the real product. No stock footage, no talking heads.
- English subtitles, large enough to read on a phone.
- No one under 18 appears or is heard.
- If any image or clip is AI-generated, it carries a visible "AI-generated" mark.
- No NASA logo.
- Hosted where it opens with no login. Check in a private window.
- Check the file's length. 0:31 is ineligible.

Slides instead of video are allowed (seven at most, title included). A video of the
working product is stronger for software.

## The local pitch

Until the organisers publish the format, prepare for three minutes plus questions.

1. **The person** (20 s). An astronaut, months from Earth, feeling slightly off.
2. **The problem** (20 s). They must notice it themselves. The challenge's own words.
3. **The product, live** (90 s). The one path. Real screen, not slides.
4. **Why it is right** (30 s). NASA's limits, by ID. NASA's data, by name. What is simulated.
5. **Why it matters** (20 s). In space, and for people far from a doctor on Earth.

Questions to have answers for:

- Is this real astronaut data? *(No. Individual medical records are private. The limits and
  the environment data are NASA's; the sample crew is simulated and labelled.)*
- How do you know the thresholds are right? *(They are NASA-STD-3001's, with the IDs.)*
- What happens with no connection to Earth?
- What did you build this weekend, and what existed before? *(Everything was built this
  weekend. The libraries are listed.)*
- Where did you use AI?
- What would you do next?

## The project page

Draft it on Saturday night; finish it on Sunday evening. Fields and limits are in
`../00-rules/submission-requirements.md`.

- **Summary:** three sentences. What it is. How it answers the challenge. Why it matters.
- **Description:** short paragraphs with headings: problem, what it does, how it works,
  NASA data, what is real and what is simulated, what is next.
- **NASA Data:** one line per dataset: name, link, what it does in the product.
- **Other data:** every library and source.
- **AI:** from the running list.
