# What was researched, and how

01 / Research method

This is a purpose-led scoping review and acquisition audit. It is not an exhaustive systematic review, a claim to PhD-level peer review, or a clinical validation study. Each source is retained because it can change a requirement, a data contract, a test case or a technology decision.

The search topics were: mission medical autonomy and roles; named challenge health outcomes; public human and analog data; acquisition devices and clocks; interoperability and privacy; crew interaction and automation; and current challenge eligibility. Primary NASA/agency publications, data repositories, standards bodies and vendor-owned interfaces were preferred.

Eligibility requires a stated purpose, identifiable publisher, useful evidence or bytes, population/context, access terms and a reproducible locator. A metadata record may qualify as evidence of a gap without qualifying as measurement data. Marketing claims and model outputs are not clinical ground truth.

The collector records requested/final URL, retrieval time, response metadata, size and SHA-256. It checks file signatures, archive integrity and publisher hashes where available. Profiling reports schemas, row grain, missingness, timing, units and cohort limits. Hash agreement establishes byte identity, not physiological truth.

- Acquisition plans and results are retained separately from interpretation.
- Current search actions were exploratory; no exhaustive search hit count or retrospective PRISMA flow is claimed.
- Stop broad collection when new sources no longer change a decision. Reopen research for a named unresolved question.
