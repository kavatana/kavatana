# Crew interface research, not a visual guess

11 / UX and usability protocol

Design around the crew's current task: what needs attention, why, what evidence is missing, what is permitted next, and who acts afterward. Keep identity, data freshness, communication state and automation mode visible. Use stable navigation and disclose detail on demand.

NASA interface guidance supports data-status cues, consistent labels, automation transparency and timely control feedback. V2 10020 covers stale/missing/unavailable status; V2 10022 gives operation-specific response times. These are not a mandate for decorative holograms, NASA logos or a model to finish a complex answer in 0.1 seconds.

Test on the team's actual laptop and landscape tablet once inventoried. Capture OS, browser/runtime, viewport, touch/keyboard use, offline storage limits and measured memory. Historical NASA device examples cannot establish the exact device for a future mission.

Proposed usability study: compare a structured evidence card against the same card with AI assistance using scripted, simulated cases. Counterbalance order, record completion, interpretation errors, missed uncertainty, unnecessary actions, time and NASA-TLX workload. Get consent and avoid collecting participants' personal health data.

- Include interrupted review, stale data, uncertain units and delayed ground response.
- Use non-color status cues and keyboard/touch checks; WCAG is a proposed baseline, not proof of spaceflight suitability.
- Representative test users are not a substitute for astronaut validation.
