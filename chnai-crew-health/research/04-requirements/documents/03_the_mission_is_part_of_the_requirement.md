# The mission is part of the requirement

03 / Concept of operations

Working scenario: a simulated long-duration deep-space transit with delayed or unavailable ground communication. This is a product hypothesis, not a claim about a selected NASA vehicle. The first user is a crew member reviewing their own health; an assigned crew medical officer and authorized ground clinicians support the episode.

Onboard autonomy is needed because medical response cannot always wait for a ground conversation. The software should reduce collection, interpretation and coordination burden while preserving the authority of trained people and mission procedures. Replacing every human decision is not an objective supported by the challenge.

A mission profile must state duration, gravity transitions, crew skills, available instruments and medications, evacuation options, communication scenarios, task schedule and privacy arrangements. Without those facts, alerting and action logic cannot be treated as operational policy.

The role register separates medical care, command operations, exercise support, radiation interpretation, biomedical technical support and data stewardship. These are proposed access boundaries grounded in role descriptions; they are not a NASA-approved authorization matrix.

- Crew member: own evidence, context correction and approved next steps.
- CMO: assigned assistance within training and authorized care relationship.
- Ground clinician: released case context and an explicit review question.
- Commander and technical support: purpose-limited operational or device status.
