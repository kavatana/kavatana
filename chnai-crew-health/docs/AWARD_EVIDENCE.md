# Lessons from official Space Apps winner descriptions

Research date: 21 October 2026. The requested awards index and three linked project pages returned 502 errors during this review. The comparisons below use NASA's accessible official winner announcements. We did not inspect those teams' code, reproduce their metrics, observe private judging, or verify the current 2026 scoring rules.

## What the source actually establishes

| Winner | Published description | Our inference and application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WMPGang, 2024 Best Use of Science | NASA describes a web app that identifies satellite risk zones using near-Earth-object and meteor-stream data. | Make the purpose of each source visible. Our comparison links its NASA requirement, baseline and original measurement context. |
| 42 QuakeHeroes, 2024 Best Use of Technology | NASA describes neural-network seismic detection with signal processing. | Advanced computation should answer a defined problem. Any future CHNAI model must improve a measured task over the existing deterministic baseline. |
| NVS-knot, 2024 Galactic Impact | NASA describes planting-risk support from soil moisture and evapotranspiration. | Connect evidence to a user's next decision. Our next-action card identifies a responsible reviewer and retains follow-up. |
| Spacebee, 2023 Best Use of Technology | NASA describes Apollo moonquake locations, types, dates and plots in an integrated website. | Preserve data context while making it accessible. Our research chart retains source rows, units and relative labels. |
| LunarTech Ensemble, 2023 Best Use of Science | NASA describes a website and immersive game for understanding Apollo seismic observations. | A simulation can explain science. Our scenario lab makes acquisition and transport failures inspectable; a future 3D view must share its state engine. |

## Evidence a judge can actually inspect in v0.5

| Product claim | Observable proof | Remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gathers evidence automatically | Run a scenario; a completed fictional assessment arrives once despite repeated delivery. | Real device contracts and end-to-end hardware validation. |
| Evaluates with explicit context | Baseline/current comparison, exact boundary checks, missing-context states and named NASA source. | Qualified clinical interpretation and applicable mission protocol. |
| Leads to accountable action | Crew request, onboard review, named follow-up owner and recorded outcome. | Health/life-science reviewer and crew usability feedback. |
| Uses actual NASA-hosted research | Two pinned OSD-569 files, source-row inspection and 28 unit-label disagreements. | Additional useful adapters, rather than a claim that all collected material is implemented. |
| Handles communication constraints | Approved packets wait, modeled transit can fail/retry, and receipt never closes care. | Actual offline deployment, multi-device synchronization, authentication and durable backend. |
| Supports repeatable engineering checks | Six selectable replays plus 12 isolated in-app checks and the Node suite. | Clinical, security, load and real hardware qualification. |

## One sentence and a proposed short demonstration

**CHNAI turns health measurements into traceable onboard review and follow-up, even when Earth is unavailable.**

That is the intended product promise. The current release demonstrates it using fictional crew and modeled links, with a separate real-research workspace. Do not describe it as deployed astronaut care.

Suggested 30-second storyboard, pending confirmation of the 2026 submission rules:

1. 0–6 seconds: run the scenario and show an assessment arriving automatically.
2. 6–12 seconds: inspect the personal comparison, original evidence and named source.
3. 12–21 seconds: show review ownership, an approved packet and its retained queue state during an outage.
4. 21–27 seconds: show simulated receipt while follow-up stays pending.
5. 27–30 seconds: state what is simulated and what comes from NASA; point to the research quality report and test evidence.

This is a storyboard, not a recorded or time-tested video. Rehearse it after clinical/story review. Keep awards strategy out of the astronaut-facing interface.
